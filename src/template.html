THE METHOD

A fast path from volume to scattering.

Uniform random points fill the enclosed model. For a single particle of constant scattering-length density, the normalized intensity is:

P(q) = | Σⱼ exp(i q · R rⱼ) / N |²

We rotate the same points, project them onto XY, XZ and YZ, then apply a 2D FFT to each density projection. Cloud-in-cell deposition, a 2× oversampled grid, and a window correction reduce gridding error. The central pixel is exactly 1.

At fixed detector size, rotation and deposition scale as O(N); the three FFTs add O(M² log M), where M is the detector width. Initial mesh checks and volume sampling have a separate, geometry-dependent cost.

The isotropic average

The dark curve averages intensity over directions on a sphere, rather than averaging the three plane curves. The default Quick setting uses 40 approximately uniformly spaced Fibonacci directions over the sphere, with equal weights. It averages intensity from all N original volume points in each direction, without a point-pair sum. Each direction is calculated with an eight-fold oversampled 1D projection FFT. The denser Fast, Balanced and Fine settings use Gauss–Legendre polar weights and evenly spaced azimuths over the positive hemisphere, exploiting I(q) = I(−q).

This numerical spherical average is cached in the original model frame over the entire qD range, up to 120. Rotation, zoom, q range, detector resolution and physical units reuse the same values. A new model, resampling, point count or isotropic accuracy restarts its separate background worker. Its cost is O(KN + KG log G), where K is the chosen direction count and G = 8192. Live detector updates do not wait for it.

The isotropic average is approximate: both angular quadrature and gridding have finite resolution. Quick is intended for fast exploration. Compare it with the denser settings for high q, elongated models or sharp minima. It retains the same finite-point self terms and approximate 1/N noise floor; it is not an exact O(N²) Debye sum.

What this MVP assumes

  • One particle, uniform contrast, dilute limit, flat small-angle detector planes. No multiple scattering, Ewald-sphere curvature, polydispersity or instrument smearing.
  • STL and OBJ solid meshes only, up to 200,000 triangles / 50 MB. Closed edges are checked at 10⁻⁶ of the longest span. Self-intersections are not detected. Nested closed shells use odd–even filling and represent cavities.
  • Sampling is uniform in volume, not on the surface. The ≈1/N intensity floor is Monte Carlo noise; increase the point count to lower it. The FFT is approximate, especially near deep minima and high q.
  • The three colored plane averages are directional cross-sections; the separate dark curve is the numerical isotropic average. q = 2π / length; q is shown in the inverse of your chosen unit. The longest span sets the physical scale and is the largest original bounding-box side, not Dmax.
  • Exports contain raw normalized intensities, including values below the display color floor. There is no absolute-intensity calibration. Export profiles includes the isotropic and plane-average curves with numerical-method metadata.

References: Larsen et al., Shape2SAS (2023) · SasView orientational averaging · morphsld_sas. This is an independent implementation.
